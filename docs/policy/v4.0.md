## **1. Purpose, principles and scope**

### **1.1 Purpose**

This policy rewards employees who find, win and deliver profitable work beyond their base role. It sets out how projects are proposed and approved, how teams are formed, how bonuses are calculated and paid, and the controls that protect the company's finances.

### **1.2 Guiding principles**

  - **Open to all eligible staff.** Any eligible employee can propose a project, apply to join one, or lead one.
  - **Pay follows profit and cash.** Bonuses come from after-tax project net revenue that has actually been collected.
  - **The company comes first.** No bonus is paid in a month the company cannot afford it, except the Type C guaranteed minimum (Section 8.6).
  - **Predictable and transparent.** Every participant receives a monthly statement showing how their bonus was calculated.
  - **Fair to contributors.** People are paid for the work they did, even if a payment arrives after they move to another project.
  - **Base role first.** Project work never comes at the expense of an employee's core job, safety or quality.

### **1.3 Who is covered**

The policy applies to employees on permanent (PKWTT) and fixed-term (PKWT) contracts, full-time or part-time, in good standing. Probationary employees may take part under Section 11. Section 3 lists the full eligibility rules and exclusions.

### **1.4 What work is covered**

The policy covers **qualifying projects**: externally contracted, revenue-generating work approved by the PRC under Section 4 and given a project code.

The following are **not** qualifying projects:

  - Internal projects with no external revenue, such as internal tools, process improvement or training. The Executive Committee may recognise these through a separate innovation award.
  - Work contracted before April 1, 2026, unless the PRC brings it in under Section 15.1.
  - Warranty, defect-correction or goodwill work on a closed project. These costs are charged back to the original project (Section 9.3).
  - Routine work assigned to an employee as part of their base role, such as an account a manager allocates to them.

### **1.5 Relationship to employment terms and the law**

  - The core terms of this policy (eligibility, payment conditions, forfeiture, deferral, clawback and deductions) are set out in the Company Regulations (Peraturan Perusahaan) or Collective Labour Agreement, as PP 36/2021 Article 11 requires. Each participant also signs Annex B as an addendum to their employment agreement. If this policy conflicts with those instruments or with Indonesian manpower law, the instruments and the law prevail.
  - Project bonuses are variable, performance-based payments. They are not part of base wage or fixed allowances and are not guaranteed.
  - Nothing in this policy reduces statutory entitlements, including overtime pay, paid leave or termination benefits.
  - The company will have this policy reviewed by Indonesian labour counsel before it takes effect, and after any material change.

## **3. Eligibility**

### **3.1 Who may take part**

To propose, join or be paid for a project, an employee must:

1.  Be employed by the company on a permanent (PKWTT) or fixed-term (PKWT) contract, full-time or part-time, or be a probationary hire (Section 11).
2.  Be in good standing (Section 2) when they join a project. A notice period or written warning stops new project roles but does not stop payment of bonuses already earned (Section 10).
3.  Meet the performance standards of their base role, as confirmed by their line manager.
4.  Have their line manager's written agreement to the time they will spend on the project.
5.  Have signed the Project Participation Agreement (Annex B) as an addendum to their employment agreement.

**Part-time and fixed-term employees** take part on the same terms as full-time permanent staff. A part-time employee's allocation factor (Section 6.4) may not exceed their contracted hours as a share of full-time hours, rounded down to the nearest 0.25. A fixed-term employee may take a role only for work periods within their contract term. Section 10 sets out what is paid when the contract ends.

### **3.2 Who may not take part**

  - Outsourced and agency staff, and daily or casual workers
  - Contractors, consultants, interns and trainees
  - Employees seconded to the company from another organisation
  - Employees on an active Performance Improvement Plan, unless the PRC approves an exception in writing
  - Employees under investigation for serious misconduct, until the investigation closes
  - Employees on unpaid leave of more than 30 consecutive days, for the duration of that leave

Outsourced staff, contractors and seconded staff may still work on a project. Their cost is a direct cost and they receive no share of the bonus pool.

### **3.3 Protected leave**

Statutory paid leave does not affect eligibility. This includes annual leave, sick leave with a medical certificate, maternity and paternity leave, and other leave protected by law.

  - Bonuses for work done before the leave are paid on the normal schedule.
  - If the leave is long, the Project Lead may reassign the role. The member's days on the team stop counting from the reassignment date (Section 6.5).
  - On return, the employee may rejoin a project on the normal terms.

### **3.4 Project-level warnings are not disciplinary warnings**

Verbal and written warnings given by a Project Lead under Section 7.5 concern project performance only. They do not by themselves affect good standing. Only formal written warnings issued by HR under the Company Regulations do.

### **3.5 Verification**

  - HR confirms eligibility before the PRC approves a team member.
  - Finance re-checks employment status before each monthly payment.
  - Employees and Project Leads must tell HR and the PMO within 2 business days of any change in status, such as a resignation, a PIP or long unpaid leave.

## **4. Proposing and approving projects**

### **4.1 Who counts as the Project Initiator**

The Project Initiator is the employee who personally originated the opportunity through their own business development, network or idea. The earliest documented client contact in the CRM is the main evidence.

There is **no** Project Initiator weight when the opportunity came through company channels. Examples include website or email enquiries to the company, tenders sent to the company, referrals from management, and repeat orders under an existing contract. For these, the PRC appoints a Project Lead.

Follow-on work from a client won under this policy belongs to the employee who originates the follow-on scope. Disputes over origination go to the PRC (Section 14).

### **4.2 Approval tiers**

The level of review depends on the size and risk of the project.

|  |  |  |  |  |
| :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
| Tier | Applies when | Proposal | Who decides | Decision within |
| 1 Fast-track | Type A or B, contract value under Rp400,000,000, existing client with good payment history | Short-form (Annex A, Part 1) | Two PRC members, one from Finance | 3 business days |
| 2 Standard | Contract value Rp400,000,000 to Rp2,000,000,000, and not Tier 3 | Full proposal (Annex A) | Full PRC | 5 business days |
| 3 Major | Over Rp2,000,000,000, any Type C project, a new country or market, or unusual liability terms | Full proposal plus legal and tax review | Full PRC with the CEO present | 10 business days |

Proposers may not split one opportunity into smaller projects to reach a lower tier (Section 12.4).

### **4.3 What a proposal must contain**

Use the standard template on the intranet. A full proposal covers:

1.  **Business case.** The opportunity, client need, value to the company and fit with strategy (1 to 2 pages).
2.  **Scope.** Deliverables with acceptance criteria, what is excluded, technical standards and dependencies.
3.  **Schedule.** Start and end dates, milestones, client approval points and payment milestones. For each payment milestone, state the work period it pays for.
4.  **Team and resources.** Proposed members, roles, weights, allocation factors and time commitment, as set out in the signed Role and Weight Agreement (Section 6.7); line manager agreement; contractors; equipment, facilities and software.
5.  **Financials.** Revenue by milestone, payment terms, direct costs with a 10 to 20% contingency, estimated taxes, projected net revenue in total and per year, contracted duration, the resulting revenue-share rate (Section 5.1), bonus pool, company margin and break-even revenue. Include best, expected and worst cases.
6.  **Client.** Background, decision-makers, contract status (verbal, LOI or signed), credit check and payment history.
7.  **Risks.** Technical, schedule, financial, safety, legal and reputational risks, each with a mitigation.
8.  **Declarations.** Conflicts of interest (Section 12.2) and confirmation that no commitment has been made to the client beyond what the proposal states.

The short form for Tier 1 covers items 1, 2, 4, 5 and 8 on two pages.

### **4.4 How proposals are evaluated**

|  |  |
| :-: | :-: |
| Criterion | What the PRC checks |
| Feasibility | Technical approach, realistic schedule, team skills and certifications, equipment availability |
| Profitability | Credible revenue, complete costs, margin above the company minimum set by the CFO, acceptable risk-adjusted return, payment terms that support cash flow |
| Resources | Team capacity and line manager agreement, leadership capacity limits (Section 7.3), no clash with higher-priority work |
| Strategy | Fit with the company's markets, client relationship value, no conflict with existing clients |
| Classification | Correct project type (Section 5.1) |
| Risk and compliance | Risks identified and mitigated; no unacceptable legal, safety, tax or reputational exposure |

### **4.5 Process and outcomes**

1.  The proposer submits to prc@company.com. The PMO acknowledges within 1 business day and assigns a tracking number.
2.  The PMO checks completeness within 2 business days. Incomplete proposals are returned with a list of what is missing; the clock restarts on resubmission.
3.  A PRC reviewer, with Finance, evaluates the proposal against Section 4.4.
4.  The deciding body meets and records one of four outcomes, with reasons:

  - **Approved.** Project code, budget, type and revenue-share rate are issued.
  - **Conditionally approved.** Conditions and a deadline are stated. The project code is issued once the PMO confirms the conditions are met.
  - **Deferred.** The information needed and a new decision date are stated.
  - **Rejected.** Written reasons and guidance on whether to resubmit are given.

1.  The proposer receives the written decision within the tier's time limit.

After approval, the PMO opens the project in the finance and project systems, records the baseline budget and schedule, sets up the Team Allocation Ledger and schedules a kickoff with Finance.

### **4.6 Appeals**

A proposer may appeal a rejection or condition within 10 business days. The appeal must bring new information or show a procedural error. The Executive Committee decides within 10 business days, and its decision is final within the company.

### **4.7 Commercial authority**

Approval of a project does not give the proposer authority to bind the company.

  - Only directors or signatories authorised under the Articles of Association may sign contracts, purchase orders over the delegated limit and guarantees.
  - Quotations and price commitments must be checked by Finance before they are sent.
  - Employees must not promise a client scope, price, schedule or liability terms beyond the approved proposal.

### **4.8 Pre-award and bid costs**

  - Time spent preparing proposals and bids is part of normal working time, with line manager agreement.
  - Out-of-pocket bid costs per opportunity need prior Finance approval.
  - If the project is won, approved bid costs become direct costs of the project. If it is lost, the company absorbs them.

### **4.9 Project leadership**

The Project Initiator becomes Project Lead unless they delegate the role at proposal stage or later by amendment under Section 6.8.

## **5. Project classification and net revenue**

### **5.1 Project types**

Each project is assigned one type at approval. The type sets the leadership capacity the project uses and affects its approval tier and deferral. All types share the same revenue-share curve. Type C projects also have a guaranteed minimum project bonus (Section 8.6).

|  |  |  |  |
| :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
| Type | Covers | Examples | Capacity factor |
| A Consulting | Professional services with intellectual output only | Advisory, engineering design and analysis, feasibility studies, process optimisation, training | 0.25 |
| B Engineering services | Engineering work with moderate company investment | Installation and commissioning, custom engineering, EPC, troubleshooting, system integration | 0.5 |
| C Product development and manufacturing | Physical products needing significant company capital | Custom equipment fabrication, prototyping, manufacturing runs, proprietary product lines | 1.0 |

The company keeps the rest of each project's net revenue (60% to 90%, depending on the revenue-share rate below), which covers salaries, overheads, facilities, capital, working capital, risk and profit.

**Regressive revenue-share rate.** Each project has one revenue-share rate (y), set at approval from its projected annual net revenue (x, in rupiah):

  - For a project contracted to run 12 months or less, x is its total projected net revenue.
  - For a longer project, x is its average annual net revenue: total projected net revenue × 12 ÷ contracted duration in months.

The rate is 40% for the smallest projects and falls to 10% at a projected annual net revenue of Rp1,500,000,000. Projects above Rp1.5 billion a year get 10%.

y = \\begin{cases} \\dfrac{40\\%}{1 + 3x / 1{,}500{,}000{,}000} & x \\le \\text{Rp}1{,}500{,}000{,}000 \\\\ 10\\% & x \> \\text{Rp}1{,}500{,}000{,}000 \\end{cases}

The rate is a flat rate on the project's total net revenue, not a marginal or progressive rate. It applies to every rupiah as the company realises it (Section 8.1), for the project's whole life, including revenue earned after month 12.

**More revenue never pays less.** For a project of 12 months or less, the bonus pool is x × y = 40% × x ÷ (1 + 3x / 1,500,000,000). This rises steadily to Rp150,000,000 at Rp1.5 billion, then rises at 10% of net revenue. A longer project earns this amount for each year of its contract. For a given contract length, more net revenue always produces a larger pool, and moving revenue between months or years does not change it. The rate itself is not rounded; only bonus amounts are rounded (Section 8.5), so rounding cannot reverse this.

|  |  |  |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| Projected annual net revenue (x) | Rate (y) | Bonus pool per year if the projection is met |
| Rp100,000,000 | 33.33% | Rp33,333,333 |
| Rp250,000,000 | 26.67% | Rp66,666,666 |
| Rp500,000,000 | 20.00% | Rp100,000,000 |
| Rp750,000,000 | 16.00% | Rp120,000,000 |
| Rp1,000,000,000 | 13.33% | Rp133,333,333 |
| Rp1,500,000,000 | 10.00% | Rp150,000,000 |
| Rp2,000,000,000 | 10.00% | Rp200,000,000 |

The rate falls because larger projects draw on more company capital, management time and risk, while the team's effort does not grow in step with contract value.

**Setting and updating the rate.**

  - Projected net revenue is the expected case in the approved proposal (Section 4.3), checked by Finance. The PRC fixes the rate in its approval decision.
  - **Contracted duration** runs from the start date to final delivery in the signed client contract, rounded up to whole months. Internal delays do not lengthen it. It changes only when the client agrees a schedule change in a contract amendment.
  - If approved change orders or contract amendments move x by more than 10%, the PRC resets the rate. The new rate applies to revenue realised after the reset. Bonuses already paid are not recalculated.
  - Every 12 months, and at final reconciliation, Finance recalculates x from actual and re-forecast net revenue. If it is more than 20% above the figure used to set the rate, the rate is recalculated for the whole project. Any overpayment is recovered first from deferred and carried-forward amounts, then under Section 9.4. This stops projects being under-projected to get a higher rate.
  - Net revenue in other currencies is converted to rupiah at the rate in Document control.
  - Splitting an opportunity into smaller projects, or lengthening a contract's stated duration without a genuine client need, to get a higher rate is prohibited (Section 12.4).

**Mixed projects.** A project is classified by the activity that makes up most of its contract value. If a distinct part worth more than 30% of the contract value belongs to another type, the PRC may split it into sub-projects with separate codes and types. Sub-projects share one rate, set on their combined projected annual net revenue. Only the PRC can change a project's type.

### **5.2 Net revenue**

\\text{Net revenue} = \\text{Project revenue} - \\text{Direct costs} - \\text{Estimated taxes}

**Project revenue** is the contract value plus approved change orders, excluding VAT. Reimbursable expenses billed to the client at cost appear in both revenue and direct costs, so they have no net effect.

### **5.3 Direct costs**

Direct costs are costs incurred specifically for the project:

  - Materials, components and supplies
  - Subcontractor, consultant and other third-party fees, including contractors and seconded staff working on the project
  - Equipment rented or bought for the project
  - Use of company-owned workshops, machines and test facilities, at internal rates published annually by Finance
  - Travel, accommodation and site allowances charged to the project
  - Approved bid costs (Section 4.8)
  - Insurance, bonds, guarantees and financing charges specific to the project
  - Penalties, liquidated damages, rework and warranty costs
  - Other verified out-of-pocket costs

Salaries and benefits of employees on the team are **not** direct costs. They are covered by the company share.

### **5.4 Estimated taxes**

The company currently pays income tax under the UMKM final tax regime (PPh Final under PP 55/2022, as amended by PP 20/2026): 0.5% of gross turnover. Finance estimates each project's taxes as follows:

  - **UMKM final income tax**: 0.5% of project revenue, excluding VAT. It is charged on revenue, not profit, so it is due even on a project that makes a loss. No separate corporate income tax is estimated while the regime applies.
  - **VAT** that cannot be recovered from the client or offset, if the company is registered for VAT (PKP).
  - **Withholding taxes** on payments to subcontractors and vendors that the company bears.
  - Other taxes directly attributable to the project.

Tax that a client withholds from its payment under the UMKM regime counts as the project's final tax, not as an extra cost. Finance confirms the company's tax position each year and publishes the method in use on the intranet (Section 9.6).

**When the UMKM regime ends.** The company loses the 0.5% rate if its gross turnover exceeds Rp4.8 billion in a tax year, if its eligibility period ends, or if it chooses the normal regime. Under current rules, the 0.5% rate continues to the end of the tax year in which turnover passes Rp4.8 billion, and normal rates apply from the next tax year. Finance confirms the exact switch date with the company's tax adviser.

From the switch date:

1.  Estimated taxes change to corporate income tax on project revenue minus direct costs, at an effective rate set by Finance. The rate reflects the statutory 22% and any reduction the company qualifies for, such as the Article 31E reduction for smaller companies.
2.  The new method applies to revenue earned from the switch date, including on projects already under way. Bonuses already paid are not recalculated.
3.  Where possible, Finance gives the PRC and all participants at least 30 days' notice of the switch, with an example of the effect on bonuses.

### **5.5 Tax reconciliation**

  - While the UMKM regime applies, the 0.5% estimate equals the tax actually due, so no reconciliation is needed beyond correcting errors.
  - Under the normal regime, estimated taxes are used for interim bonuses. They are reconciled with actual taxes at project close or fiscal year-end, whichever comes first.
  - If actual taxes are **lower**, the difference is paid as an adjustment bonus in the next cycle, subject to the break-even test.
  - If actual taxes are **higher**, the company absorbs the difference. There is no clawback for tax estimate variance.
  - A variance of more than 15% is reviewed by the CFO and reported to the PRC.

### **5.6 Change orders**

The Project Lead must raise a change order for any work outside the approved scope. Approved change orders add to project revenue. Out-of-scope work done without a change order adds cost but no revenue.

### **5.7 Loss-making projects**

  - Bonuses are calculated on cumulative net revenue (Section 8.1), so early losses are recovered from later payments before any bonus is paid.
  - If a project's cumulative net revenue is zero or negative, no bonus is paid. Employees never owe money because a project lost money, except under the clawback rules in Section 9.4.
  - A loss on one project is not offset against bonuses on other projects.

## **6. Roles, weights and bonus calculation**

### **6.1 Standard roles**

|  |  |  |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| Role | Responsibilities | Weight |
| Project Initiator | Originated the opportunity; business development and client acquisition (Section 4.1) | 0.5 |
| Project Lead | Delivery, client relationship, budget, schedule, team coordination, change orders, monthly reporting | 2.0 |
| Technical Lead | Technical direction, design authority, quality assurance, technical sign-off | 2.0 |
| Specialist | Domain expertise such as QA/QC, procurement, HSE, commissioning | 1.5 to 2.5 (Section 6.2) |
| Engineer | Technical execution: analysis, design, calculations, drafting, documentation, site work | 1.0 |
| Project Secretary | Administration, scheduling, document control, meeting records, client correspondence | 1.0 |

Every project needs a Project Lead. The PRC decides at approval which other roles the scope requires.

### **6.2 Specialist weights**

The team agrees each Specialist's weight within this range in the Role and Weight Agreement (Section 6.7), using this guide:

  - **1.5**: supporting specialist with a defined, limited input.
  - **2.0**: key specialist whose work is central to a deliverable.
  - **2.5**: accountable specialist who signs off a regulated or safety-critical deliverable, such as an engineer of record.

### **6.3 Holding more than one role**

Weights add up when one person holds several roles. For example, Initiator plus Project Lead is 0.5 + 2.0 = 2.5, and Project Lead plus Technical Lead is 4.0. An Initiator who delegates the Project Lead role keeps the 0.5 Initiator weight; the new lead receives 2.0.

### **6.4 Allocation factor**

Weights assume a member gives the role the time it normally needs. When a member commits materially less, an allocation factor of 0.25, 0.5 or 0.75 is set in the Role and Weight Agreement (Section 6.7), or later by amendment (Section 6.8). The default is 1.0.

### **6.5 Team Allocation Ledger and work periods**

The PMO keeps a Team Allocation Ledger for every project. It records each member's roles, weights, allocation factor, and start and end dates on the team.

Each client payment is linked to a **work period**, the span of work it pays for:

  - For milestone payments, the work period runs from the end of the previous milestone's work period (or project start) to acceptance of this milestone.
  - For monthly or time-based billing, the work period is the period billed.
  - Where neither fits, the Project Lead proposes the work periods at setup and the PRC approves them.

A payment's bonus pool is shared among the people who were on the team during its work period, in proportion to their effective weight-days. This means people are paid for work they did even if the payment arrives after they leave the team, provided they are still eligible on the payment date (Section 10).

### **6.6 Bonus formula**

For each collected payment:

B\_i = \\frac{E\_i}{\\sum\_{j=1}^{n} E\_j} \\times Q \\qquad E\_i = W\_i \\times A\_i \\times d\_i

|  |  |
| :-: | :-: |
| Symbol | Meaning |
| B\\\_i | Bonus for employee i from this payment |
| E\\\_i | Effective weight-days of employee i in the work period |
| W\\\_i | Combined role weight of employee i (Sections 6.1 to 6.3) |
| A\\\_i | Allocation factor of employee i (default 1.0) |
| d\\\_i | Days employee i was on the team during the work period |
| n | Number of eligible employees on the team during the work period |
| Q | The payment's share of the bonus pool: the project's revenue-share rate × the payment's net revenue, after recovering any earlier shortfall (Sections 5.1 and 8.1) |

When everyone is on the team for the whole work period at an allocation factor of 1.0, the formula reduces to weight divided by total weight, as in Section 16.1.

### **6.7 Agreeing roles and weights before the project starts**

Roles and weights must be agreed before any project work begins. No work counts toward a bonus until this agreement is in place.

  - The proposed team signs a Role and Weight Agreement (Annex D) setting each member's role, weight and allocation factor.
  - The standard weights in Section 6.1 apply unless every initial member agrees in writing to different weights that reflect actual responsibilities.
  - The agreement is submitted with the proposal. At approval, the PRC checks it for eligibility, leadership capacity and line manager agreement.
  - The PMO records the agreement in the Team Allocation Ledger.

### **6.8 Amending roles and weights during the project**

Roles and weights may change at any point in the project, for example when someone joins or leaves, the Project Lead role is delegated, or responsibilities shift.

1.  Any team member may propose an amendment in writing to the whole team, stating the change and the date it takes effect.
2.  Every current team member has one vote, regardless of weight. The amendment passes if more than half of all current members vote for it within 5 business days.
3.  If exactly half vote for it, the PRC casts the deciding vote within 5 business days.
4.  A person joining the team does not vote on their own admission, but must consent to their role, weight and allocation factor.
5.  The Project Lead sends the result to the PMO, which updates the ledger and notifies the PRC. The PRC may block an amendment only if it breaks this policy, for example on eligibility, leadership capacity or line manager agreement.
6.  Amendments apply from their effective date forward. They never change weights for work already done or for work periods already paid.

A member whose own role is removed, or whose weight is reduced, by an amendment they voted against may appeal to the PRC under Section 14. Removal for poor performance follows Section 7.5 instead.

## **7. Workload, capacity and performance**

### **7.1 Base role first**

  - Each member's total planned project time is agreed with their line manager and recorded in the Project Participation Agreement.
  - A member may hold up to **3 concurrent non-lead roles** (Engineer, Secretary or Specialist). More needs line manager and PRC approval.
  - Lead roles are limited by the leadership capacity score in Section 7.3, and also count toward the time agreed with the line manager.
  - If project work starts to affect the base role, the line manager may ask the PRC to reduce the member's project load.

### **7.2 Working time and overtime**

Project work is normally done within working hours. Overtime follows the Company Regulations and Indonesian law, and must be approved in advance. Project bonuses do not replace statutory overtime pay.

### **7.3 Leadership capacity**

Each Project Lead or Technical Lead role uses capacity according to the project type. An employee's total may not exceed **2.0** at any time.

|  |  |  |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| Project type | Capacity factor | Maximum if all leads are this type |
| A Consulting | 0.25 | 8 projects |
| B Engineering services | 0.5 | 4 projects |
| C Product development | 1.0 | 2 projects |

Holding both Project Lead and Technical Lead on the same project counts that project's factor once. Section 16.3 gives worked examples.

### **7.4 Performance monitoring**

Each month the Project Lead rates every member on deliverable quality and timeliness, communication, technical contribution, and collaboration and professionalism. The rating is shared with the member and their line manager, and feeds the annual performance review.

### **7.5 Removal from a project**

If a member's project performance falls below standard:

1.  The Project Lead gives a verbal warning naming the areas to improve.
2.  If there is no improvement within 2 weeks, the Project Lead gives a written project warning with a 1-week improvement period.
3.  If performance still falls short, the Project Lead may remove the member with PRC approval.
4.  The removed member keeps their share of payments for work periods up to the removal date, reduced in proportion to any work that had to be redone.
5.  The removal is recorded and reported to HR and the line manager.
6.  The member may appeal to the PRC within 5 business days.

These project warnings are not disciplinary warnings (Section 3.4). Serious misconduct is handled under the Company Regulations instead.

## **8. Bonus payment cycle**

### **8.1 Bonuses follow collected cash, cumulatively**

Interim bonuses are based on revenue that is both **earned** and **collected**: the related work has been delivered (milestone accepted or period billed) and the client's payment has reached the company's bank account. Advance payments and down payments count only once the work they relate to is delivered.

For each payment the company realises, Finance calculates:

1.  Cumulative net revenue to date: collected revenue, minus direct costs incurred or accrued, minus estimated taxes.
2.  The **cumulative pool**: the project's revenue-share rate (Section 5.1) × cumulative net revenue.
3.  The payment's pool: the cumulative pool minus pool already allocated. If the result is negative, the payment's pool is zero and the shortfall is recovered from later payments.

The payment's pool is shared among the members on the team during its work period (Section 6.5). After a rate reset (Section 5.1), the cumulative pool is the pool at the old rate up to the reset plus the new rate × net revenue realised after it. Type C top-ups (Section 8.6) do not count as pool already allocated; they are deducted only through each member's top-up balance.

### **8.2 When bonuses are paid**

Bonuses are paid with the next salary payment after the company realises the revenue, usually on the 25th of the month.

  - **Cut-off.** Revenue collected by the 20th is paid with that month's salary. Revenue collected after the 20th is paid with the following month's salary.
  - **By the 22nd.** Finance calculates provisional bonuses. Each Project Lead confirms the Team Allocation Ledger.
  - **By the 23rd.** The CFO certifies the break-even test and cash check (Section 9.1), using the latest three closed months. The PRC authorises release in full, or applies Section 9.2 if a check fails.
  - **Salary date, usually the 25th.** Authorised bonuses are paid through payroll and every participant receives a bonus statement.

If the salary date moves for a weekend or public holiday, bonuses move with it. The other dates move to the previous business day.

### **8.3 Project close and final reconciliation**

A project closes when all deliverables are accepted, the final invoice is paid or written off, and final costs are booked. The Project Lead then submits a close-out report covering acceptance, final financials, client feedback and lessons learned.

Within 30 days of close, Finance recalculates the total pool using final figures, including actual taxes where they are lower than estimated (Section 5.5) and any rate recalculation under Section 5.1:

  - If the final pool is **at least** the amount already paid, the difference is paid with the next salary.
  - If the final pool is **lower**, the overpayment is taken first from deferred and carried-forward amounts. Any remainder is recovered under Section 9.4 only if it results from a revenue reversal, misconduct or a data error; otherwise the company absorbs it.
  - If a final invoice is unpaid 90 days after its due date, the project may be closed without it. If the money is collected later, a late-collection bonus is paid to eligible members.

### **8.4 Bonus statements**

Every participant receives a monthly statement for each project showing collected revenue, direct costs, estimated taxes, net revenue, the pool, their ledger share, amounts deferred or carried forward, and the amount paid. Errors must be raised with Finance within 30 days of the statement date (Section 14).

### **8.5 Payment, tax and rounding**

  - Bonuses are paid in rupiah through payroll, with income tax (PPh 21) withheld as required by law.
  - Amounts are rounded down to the nearest whole rupiah: each payment's pool first, then each member's share. Rounding differences stay with the company.
  - Underpayments found by Finance or raised by an employee are corrected in the next cycle.

### **8.6 Guaranteed minimum project bonus for Type C projects**

Type C projects often run for months before they earn revenue. Each Type C project therefore has a guaranteed minimum project bonus: a minimum monthly bonus pool (the **standard bonus**) paid whatever revenue the project makes. The standard bonus is at least Rp1,000,000 a month. A higher amount may be proposed in the Role and Weight Agreement (Annex D), and the PRC fixes the amount at approval. The PRC may agree to raise it at kickoff, recorded as an amendment to the Role and Weight Agreement. After kickoff it stays fixed for the life of the project.

1.  **Top-up.** In each salary cycle (Section 8.2), if the project's revenue-based pool (Section 8.1) is less than the standard bonus, the company tops it up to the standard bonus. The minimum applies from the month project work starts. The first month is pro-rated by days.
2.  **Sharing.** The revenue-based part of the month's pool is shared by work period as usual (Section 6.5). The top-up is shared among the members on the team that month, in proportion to their effective weight-days in the month (Section 6.6).
3.  **Probationary employees.** In any month in which a top-up is paid, probationary employees are paid their share of that month's pool with salary rather than having it held. Their revenue-based bonuses in other months are held as normal (Section 11).
4.  **Tax.** Top-ups are bonus payments, taxed through PPh 21 when paid. They are not loans.
5.  **Deduction from later bonuses.** The PMO records each member's share of every top-up as their top-up balance on the project. That member's later revenue-based bonuses from the same project are reduced by the balance until it is cleared:

<!-- end list -->

  - While the minimum still applies, only each member's share of the pool above the standard bonus is used, so the team still receives at least the standard bonus that month.
  - From the month in which the minimum ends, each member's bonuses from the project are reduced in full until their balance is cleared.

<!-- end list -->

1.  **Balance not cleared.** Deductions come only from bonuses on the same project. Any balance left when the project closes, or when the member leaves the company, lapses. It is never recovered from salary or final pay.
2.  **When it ends.** The minimum stops permanently after either:

<!-- end list -->

  - the revenue-based pool is more than the standard bonus in three consecutive months; or
  - the revenue-based pool in a single month is 12 times the standard bonus or more.

<!-- end list -->

1.  **Paid even when a check fails.** The standard bonus is paid in full every month, even in a month the break-even test or cash check fails (Section 9.2). It still counts as a proposed bonus when the checks are run, so it can reduce what other bonuses are paid that month. Only revenue-based amounts above the standard bonus are paid pro rata and carried forward.

## **9. Financial controls**

### **9.1 Monthly break-even test and cash check**

No bonus is payable until the company passes both checks below and the PRC authorises release.

**Break-even test.** Calculated on a rolling three months (the month just closed and the two before it) to smooth lumpy milestone revenue:

\\text{Operating result} = \\text{Company revenue} - \\text{Direct project costs} - \\text{Operating overheads} - \\text{Proposed project bonuses}

  - **Company revenue**: all revenue on the accounting (accrual) basis, excluding VAT, including work outside this policy.
  - **Direct project costs**: as defined in Section 5.3, for all projects.
  - **Operating overheads**: all other operating costs, including every employee's salary and benefits, rent, utilities, depreciation, insurance and interest.
  - **Proposed project bonuses**: all bonuses due for release this month, including amounts carried forward.

The test is passed when the operating result is zero or more. Gross revenue is used, not project net revenue, so that direct costs and taxes are not deducted twice.

**Cash check.** After paying the proposed bonuses, the company's cash must be at least **two months** of fixed operating costs. The CFO may propose a different floor to the Executive Committee each year.

The CFO certifies both results to the PRC by the 23rd. Workings are kept for audit.

### **9.2 If either check fails**

1.  **Pay pro rata.** The PRC releases the largest share of proposed bonuses that still passes both checks, after paying Type C standard bonuses in full (Section 8.6). Every employee receives the same percentage of their bonus.
2.  **Carry forward the rest.** Unpaid amounts are carried forward and paid first in the next month both checks pass, before new bonuses. Carried-forward amounts are not forfeited because of the failed test; Section 10 still applies if an employee's status changes.
3.  **Review after three months.** If the checks fail three months in a row, the PRC reviews the project portfolio and may propose a revenue-share change under Section 9.5.

The revenue-share curve is never cut retroactively, or without notice, in response to a failed test.

### **9.3 Deferral for high-risk projects**

**When it applies.** The PRC applies deferral at approval if any of these is true:

  - Projected total net revenue is over Rp1,000,000,000.
  - It is a Type C project with a warranty period.
  - Payment terms are longer than 90 days.
  - The client is new, with no payment history with the company.
  - The PRC rates the technical or execution risk as high.

**How it works.**

  - Up to 30% of each interim bonus is deferred for 6 to 12 months after project close. The PRC sets the percentage and the period at approval, based on the warranty period and risk. The rest is paid with salary. Deferral never takes a Type C project's monthly pool below its standard bonus (Section 8.6).
  - Warranty, rework and other costs that arise during the deferral period are deducted from the deferred pool. Costs beyond the deferred pool are absorbed by the company.
  - Amounts linked to unpaid invoices are held until the invoices are paid. If an invoice is written off, the deferred pool is reduced by the bonus that invoice generated.
  - If final net revenue is more than 10% below the approved forecast, the PRC reviews the reasons. If the shortfall came from figures misstated at approval, it may reduce the deferred amount by up to the percentage shortfall.
  - The remaining deferred amount is paid in the cycle after the deferral period ends, subject to Section 10. It earns no interest.

### **9.4 Clawback**

The company may recover bonuses already paid if, within 24 months of payment:

  - **Revenue is reversed**, through client default, bad-debt write-off, contract cancellation or a scope reduction.
  - **Misconduct is proven** under the Company Regulations: fraud, gross negligence, an undisclosed conflict of interest or serious misconduct connected with the project.
  - **Data errors** in revenue, costs or the ledger led to an overpayment. This includes an overpayment left after a rate recalculation (Section 5.1).

**Amount.** Bonus paid minus the bonus recalculated on corrected figures. No penalties or interest are added.

**Order of recovery.** The company recovers first from deferred and carried-forward amounts, then from future project bonuses, and only then from salary.

**Process.**

1.  Finance identifies the trigger and calculates the amount.
2.  The CFO presents the case to the PRC, which decides by majority vote.
3.  Affected employees receive written notice with the calculation.
4.  Employees have 10 business days to dispute the figures or raise mitigating factors.
5.  Salary deductions need the employee's specific written agreement for each recovery, are spread over 6 to 12 months, and may not exceed 20% of monthly gross salary. Total deductions from any wage payment, including this one, never exceed 50% of that payment.
6.  For employees who have left, any balance may be set off against final pay only with the employee's written agreement in the separation agreement. Otherwise the company may pursue civil recovery.

**Protections.**

  - No clawback for normal business or market risk, including cost overruns, rework, warranty claims, penalties and legal settlements. These are absorbed through deferral (Section 9.3) or by the company.
  - Where a data error was not caused by the employee, the first Rp10,000,000 of overpayment per person per project is not clawed back. Only the amount above Rp10,000,000 is recovered.
  - Every clawback decision is recorded with its reasons.

### **9.5 Changing the revenue-share curve**

The PRC may change the curve's starting rate, floor rate or decay point when profitability declines for three consecutive months, quarterly targets are at risk, major unforeseen costs or economic conditions arise, or long-term investment needs capital. Each parameter must stay within the limits below, which are set in the Company Regulations, and the starting rate must be higher than the floor rate. With starting rate S, floor rate F and decay point D, the rate is y = S ÷ (1 + (S ÷ F − 1) × x ÷ D), which keeps the pool rising with net revenue for any values.

|  |  |  |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| Parameter | Current | Allowed range |
| Starting rate (at Rp0) | 40% | 20% to 60% |
| Floor rate (at and above the decay point) | 10% | 5% to 30% |
| Decay point | Rp1,500,000,000 | Rp500,000,000 to Rp5,000,000,000 |

1.  The CFO presents the analysis and a recommendation.
2.  The PRC approves or modifies it.
3.  Employees receive at least 30 days' written notice, with the reasons and financial context.
4.  The new curve applies only to projects approved after the effective date. Projects already approved keep their curve. Changes outside the limits above need an amendment to the Company Regulations (Section 15.3).

### **9.6 Transparency**

  - The current revenue-share curve, thresholds and tax-estimation method are posted on the intranet, with a history of changes.
  - Every month, all employees receive a company financial summary (revenue, costs, margin) and the break-even test result. Detailed figures go to the PRC only.
  - Quarterly town halls review financial performance and explain any changes.
  - Deferral, carry-forward, clawback and probationary balances are reported in aggregate. Individual amounts are confidential.

## **11. Probationary employees**

New full-time hires on probation may take part fully, but their bonuses are held until probation ends.

### **11.1 Who this applies to**

New hires on permanent (PKWTT) contracts during their probation period, which by law is a maximum of 3 months and cannot be extended. Fixed-term (PKWT) contracts cannot include probation. Existing employees on a Performance Improvement Plan are covered by Section 3.2 instead; if the PRC approves their participation, they are paid normally.

### **11.2 Participation**

  - Probationary employees may hold any role suited to their skills and capacity, subject to the normal performance standards.
  - Their bonuses are calculated with the standard formula and included in the project pool.
  - Before joining their first project, they sign a written acknowledgment that their bonuses are held until probation ends.
  - The Project Lead is told when a probationary employee joins the team.

### **11.3 How bonuses are held**

  - All authorised bonuses are held, not paid, during probation, except their share of a Type C project's pool in any month a top-up is paid, which is paid with salary (Section 8.6).
  - Finance records held amounts in a separate Probationary Bonus Accrual account as a company liability.
  - Each month the employee receives a statement showing projects, amounts calculated, the cumulative balance held and the expected probation end date.
  - Holding a probationary employee's bonus does not delay or reduce other team members' payments.

### **11.4 Outcome**

## **12. Integrity, conduct and professional standards**

A bonus is never a reason to cut corners. Breaches of this section can lead to removal from projects, clawback (Section 9.4) and disciplinary action under the Company Regulations, including dismissal with forfeiture of unpaid bonuses for a serious violation (Section 10).

### **12.1 Clients and opportunities belong to the company**

  - All clients, contracts, revenue, data and work product of a project belong to the company.
  - Employees must not divert an opportunity away from the company, or do paid work for a project client or supplier outside the company, without written approval from the CEO.
  - A client relationship does not transfer with an employee who leaves.

### **12.2 Conflicts of interest**

  - Before approval, and whenever a new conflict arises, team members must disclose any family, financial or business relationship with the client, a supplier or a subcontractor.
  - The PRC decides how to manage each conflict. It may change the team, require an independent check of prices, or reject the project.
  - An undisclosed conflict found later is treated as misconduct (Section 9.4).

### **12.3 Anti-bribery and fair dealing**

  - No one may offer, give, request or accept a bribe, kickback or improper benefit to win or run a project. This covers public officials, client staff, suppliers and their relatives.
  - Gifts, hospitality and sponsorships follow the company gifts policy. Facilitation payments are prohibited.
  - Bid-rigging, price-fixing and sharing confidential tender information are prohibited.

### **12.4 Integrity of project accounting**

No one may manipulate project figures to increase or bring forward a bonus. In particular, employees must not:

  - Split one opportunity into smaller projects to reach a lower approval tier, avoid deferral or get a higher revenue-share rate.
  - Book costs to the wrong project, or move team time or materials between projects.
  - Propose a project type that does not match the work.
  - Pressure a client to pay early, or delay invoices, to shift bonuses between months or around a status change.
  - Underprice work, understate risks or understate projected net revenue or overstate a contract's duration, to win approval or a higher revenue-share rate.

Finance has final authority over cost allocation and revenue recognition.

### **12.5 Confidentiality and intellectual property**

Designs, calculations, software, drawings and other project work are company property, subject to the client contract. Client information is confidential and used only for the project.

### **12.6 Safety and professional standards**

  - Site and workshop work follows the company occupational health and safety (K3) system and the client's site rules.
  - Work that law or contract reserves for certified people, such as signing regulated designs, is done only by staff with the required certification.
  - Deliverables pass the company's technical review before release.
  - A serious safety breach by a team member may lead to removal from the project and disciplinary action under the Company Regulations.

### **12.7 Speaking up**

Anyone who suspects a breach of this policy should report it to HR, the CFO or the company's whistleblowing channel. Reports made in good faith are protected from retaliation.

## **13. Governance, responsibilities and records**

### **13.1 Project Review Committee**

**Members.** The CEO (President Director) as chair, the CFO, the COO, and up to two senior technical managers appointed by the CEO for two-year terms. The PMO acts as secretary and does not vote.

**Board of Commissioners.** Commissioners supervise rather than manage the company, so they do not sit on the PRC. They receive a quarterly report on the project portfolio, bonuses paid, deferrals, clawbacks and policy exceptions.

**CEO veto.** The CEO may veto any PRC decision, including approvals, revenue-share changes, disputes, removals and exceptions. Every veto is recorded in writing with reasons and reported to the Board of Commissioners each quarter.

## **14. Disputes and appeals**

Disputes may concern origination, role assignment, ledger entries, bonus calculations, performance ratings, removals, probation, forfeiture or clawback.

1.  **Raise it in time.** Calculation and ledger disputes must be raised within 30 days of the statement date. Other disputes must be raised within 30 days of the decision or event.
2.  **Try to resolve it directly.** The parties, with the Project Lead or Finance as relevant, try to settle it within 10 business days.
3.  **Escalate to the PRC.** The PRC decides within 10 business days of escalation. Members involved in the dispute are recused.
4.  **Appeal to the Executive Committee.** Either party may appeal within 10 business days. The Executive Committee decides within 10 business days, and its decision is final within the company.

This process does not limit an employee's rights under Indonesian industrial relations law, including bipartite negotiation.

### **16.4 A failed break-even test**

Rolling three-month figures: company revenue Rp1,200,000,000, direct project costs Rp380,000,000, operating overheads Rp690,000,000, proposed bonuses Rp160,000,000.

Operating result = Rp1,200,000,000 − Rp380,000,000 − Rp690,000,000 − Rp160,000,000 = −Rp30,000,000. The test fails.

The largest payout that passes is Rp130,000,000, which is 81.25% of proposed bonuses. Every employee receives 81.25% of their bonus this month. The remaining Rp30,000,000 is carried forward and paid first in the next month both checks pass.

### **16.5 Probationary employee**

  - **If confirmed on April 1:** he receives Rp21,900,000 as a Probation Completion Bonus by April 30, less PPh 21. Later bonuses are paid with his monthly salary.
  - **If probation is not passed:** the Rp21,900,000 is paid with his final pay, less PPh 21, because it was earned for work already done.

### **16.7 Multi-year project**

A Type B project is contracted for 24 months, with projected net revenue of Rp1,600,000,000: Rp700,000,000 in the first 12 months and Rp900,000,000 in the second. Its annual net revenue is Rp1,600,000,000 × 12 ÷ 24 = Rp800,000,000, so its rate is 40% ÷ (1 + 3 × 800,000,000 ÷ 1,500,000,000) = 15.385% (rounded for display). The rate applies to all of the project's net revenue.

The total is the same however the revenue is split between the two years, so there is nothing to gain by moving milestones.

### **16.8 Type C guaranteed minimum**

A Type C fabrication project starts work on January 1. It earns little revenue at first, so the minimum pool applies. Its standard bonus is set at the minimum of Rp1,000,000.

|  |  |  |  |  |  |  |
| :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
| Month | Revenue-based pool | Top-up | Top-up deducted | Paid to team | Top-ups still to deduct | Months in a row above Rp1,000,000 |
| January | Rp0 | Rp1,000,000 | Rp0 | Rp1,000,000 | Rp1,000,000 | 0 |
| February | Rp0 | Rp1,000,000 | Rp0 | Rp1,000,000 | Rp2,000,000 | 0 |
| March | Rp0 | Rp1,000,000 | Rp0 | Rp1,000,000 | Rp3,000,000 | 0 |
| April | Rp400,000 | Rp600,000 | Rp0 | Rp1,000,000 | Rp3,600,000 | 0 |
| May | Rp3,000,000 | Rp0 | Rp2,000,000 | Rp1,000,000 | Rp1,600,000 | 1 |
| June | Rp800,000 | Rp200,000 | Rp0 | Rp1,000,000 | Rp1,800,000 | 0 |
| July | Rp2,000,000 | Rp0 | Rp1,000,000 | Rp1,000,000 | Rp800,000 | 1 |
| August | Rp1,500,000 | Rp0 | Rp500,000 | Rp1,000,000 | Rp300,000 | 2 |
| September | Rp4,000,000 | Rp0 | Rp300,000 | Rp3,700,000 | Rp0 | 3 |

The team keeps the same members and weights throughout, so the team figures equal the sum of each member's own top-up balance. While the minimum applies, only the pool above Rp1,000,000 is used for deductions, so the team never receives less than Rp1,000,000 a month. The company paid Rp3,800,000 in top-ups, and all of it was deducted from later bonuses by September. The minimum ends after September, the third month in a row above Rp1,000,000. Had May's pool been Rp12,000,000, the minimum would have ended in May, Rp3,600,000 would have been deducted from it, and the team would have received Rp8,400,000.

A probationary engineer who joins this team on April 1 with a three-month probation is paid their share with salary in April and June, the months with a top-up. Their May share, after earlier top-ups are deducted, is held until probation ends on June 30.

## **Annex H. Internal rates for company workshops and equipment**

Published by Finance at the start of each financial year (Section 5.3). Projects are charged these rates as direct costs for using company-owned facilities and equipment. Rates cover depreciation, maintenance, consumables and the time of operators who are not on the project team, where stated, and are reviewed against market rental prices.
